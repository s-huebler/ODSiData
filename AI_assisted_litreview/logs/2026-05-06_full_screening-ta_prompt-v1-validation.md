## Session Summary

Built and validated the first-pass title/abstract screening prompt for the
ODSiData scoping review. v1 was distilled from a 13-paper training set with
reviewer gold-standard labels, then validated against a 10-record holdout
(Screen2). Agreement was 9/10 (kappa = 0.74). The lone disagreement involved
a skin-microbiome paper that also reported confirmed intestinal dysbiosis;
the reviewer ruled this should pass first-pass filtering, and the rule was
encoded into prompt v2 by adding "gut/intestinal dysbiosis" as a qualifying
signal in criterion 2 and adding a carve-out for non-gut-primary papers with
observed gut dysbiosis. The next step is to re-validate v2 on a fresh holdout
before applying it to the full screening pool.

## Records / Outputs

### Training set (Initial_Training.xlsx, n=13)

Used to derive the rubric. All 13 reviewer labels were treated as gold
standard; no AI labels were produced for these. Patterns extracted:

- Three required dimensions (cohort, gut microbiome exposure, GVHD outcome).
- Reviews must give the gut microbiome a substantive mechanistic role, not a
  passing mention as part of the gut tissue environment (Row 9 vs Row 10
  distinction in the training set).
- Non-gut microbiome sites alone (e.g., vaginal — Row 8) do not qualify.
- Mention of dysbiosis in a list of factors does not qualify (Rows 4, 6, 13).
- Missing/degenerate abstract → defer to title (Row 3).
- First-pass leniency: lean Include when uncertain.

### Disagreement detail (Row 1)

Title: "Disturbances in microbial skin recolonization and cutaneous immune
response following allogeneic stem cell transfer."

Key abstract sentence: "We could confirm intestinal dysbiosis following HSCT
and report that the skin microbiome is likewise perturbed in HSCT-recipients."

AI v1 reasoning: substantive focus is skin microbiome; intestinal dysbiosis is
secondary reference, not study exposure → Discard.

Human reasoning: "Mention of confirmed intestinal dysbiosis indicates that
stool samples were taken. This is an edge case that should err on the side of
inclusion."

Resolution: Include. Encoded into v2 by adding "explicit
observation/confirmation of gut dysbiosis or intestinal dysbiosis (which
implies stool was sampled)" as a qualifying signal in criterion 2, and
adding a carve-out so that a non-gut-primary paper qualifies if it also
reports observed gut/intestinal dysbiosis.

### v1 → v2 diff (criterion 2 only)

v1: "Qualifying signals: 16S rRNA sequencing, shotgun metagenomics, fecal
microbiota transplant (FMT), prebiotic or probiotic intervention with
microbiome readout, microbial diversity metrics (alpha/beta diversity), named
gut taxa, or SCFA / butyrate / bile acid measurement as microbial metabolites.
Non-gut microbiome sites alone (vaginal, oral, skin, lung) do NOT qualify. A
passing mention of 'gut microbiota' or 'dysbiosis' within a list of factors
is NOT enough — the microbiome must be a substantive focus."

v2: "Qualifying signals: 16S rRNA sequencing, shotgun metagenomics, fecal
microbiota transplant (FMT), prebiotic or probiotic intervention with
microbiome readout, microbial diversity metrics (alpha/beta diversity), named
gut taxa, SCFA / butyrate / bile acid measurement as microbial metabolites,
**or explicit observation/confirmation of gut dysbiosis or intestinal
dysbiosis (which implies stool was sampled)**. Non-gut microbiome sites alone
(vaginal, oral, skin, lung) do NOT qualify on their own, **but a paper whose
primary site is non-gut does qualify if it also reports observed
gut/intestinal dysbiosis**. A passing mention of 'gut microbiota' or generic
'dysbiosis' within a list of factors is NOT enough — the microbiome must be
a substantive focus or an explicitly observed finding."
