# Therapeutic strategies across the four anchor reviews

Compiled 2026-11-03 from the anchor-review skills. Reference numbers are the citing
review's own numbering, so a claim can be traced back in one step.
Moses = Moses & Yeh 2026 Gut Microbes; Paredes = Paredes et al. 2026 Nat Rev Cancer;
Samarkhazan = Soleimani Samarkhazan et al. 2025 J Transl Med; Weber = Weber et al. 2026 BMT.

Moses organizes the whole landscape as three eras (decontamination, pre/probiotics,
ecosystem restoration). That spine holds for all four reviews and is used below.

## 1. Decontamination and antibiotic management

| Intervention | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Protective isolation, sterile food, non-absorbable oral antibiotics | Reduced but did not eliminate aGvHD; decontamination harder clinically than preclinically | Moses Part 1 refs 5-7, 80, 81; Supp Table 1 (Storb 1983, Skinhoj 1987) |
| Carbapenems, piperacillin/tazobactam | Associated with increased GvHD-related mortality; cefepime and aztreonam were not | Weber Antibiotic stewardship ref 56; Moses Part 1 refs 16, 82-84 |
| Imipenem-cilastatin (murine) | Degraded protective mucus layer, attributed to Akkermansia muciniphila | Weber ref 56 |
| Antibiotic timing | Antibiotics before day of transplant associated with loss of Clostridia and increased TRM | Weber ref 55 |
| Rifaximin vs ciprofloxacin/metronidazole | Retrospective n=394: lower enterococcal positivity, higher urinary 3-indoxyl sulfate, lower 1-year TRM | Moses Part 1 ref 19 |
| Rifaximin vs ciprofloxacin, prospective | n=38, no difference in grade 2+ aGvHD (44.4% vs 60%); rifaximin can breed resistant enterococci | Moses refs 85, 86 |
| Vancomycin-polymyxin B gut decontamination | Randomized phase II, 10 vs 10, no difference in diversity or GVHD outcomes | Paredes Table 2 part 1, NCT02641236, ref 109 |
| CRS-aware restrictive antibiotic guideline | More diversity, trend to less severe GI GvHD, no increase in infection | Weber ref 57 |
| Antibiotic-neutralizing agents | Beta-lactamases, polymer-coated activated charcoal, engineered probiotics; lower gut antibiotic levels without losing systemic efficacy | Weber refs 58-62; Paredes refs 115-117 |

## 2. Nutrition

| Intervention | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Enteral vs parenteral nutrition | Meta-analysis favours enteral for aGVHD and GI aGVHD incidence; no 100-day OS difference | Paredes Nutritional support ref 120 |
| Prebiotic fibre enteral nutrition | Pilot, 20 vs 10, increased lactobacillus_C rhamnosus (p=0.022), fewer antibiotic resistance genes | Weber ref 72 (Andersen) |

## 3. Prebiotics

| Intervention | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| GFO (glutamine, fibre, oligosaccharide) | Retrospective n=44, shorter oral mucositis, trend to less Enterococcus translocation | Moses ref 87 |
| Amylofiber SH (resistant starch) + GFO | Prospective n=49 vs 142 historical controls; diversity and butyrate maintained; lower grade II-IV GvHD, significant for skin but not gut or liver | Moses ref 89; Weber ref 71; Paredes ref 141 |
| Resistant potato starch (RPS) | Pilot n=10, day -7 to +100; fecal butyrate significantly higher on RPS (p<0.0001); plasma metabolite change independent of RPS | Moses ref 21; Weber ref 69 (Riwes); Paredes ref 142 |
| RPS phase II | NCT02763033 ongoing; second phase II NCT06784336 pending, primary outcome aGVHD incidence | Paredes ref 124; Moses Part 2 |
| Fructo-oligosaccharides (FOS) | Phase I dose escalation, well tolerated in allo-HCT | Paredes ref 136 |
| FamiLact (7 strains) + FOS | Pilot RCT, reduced severe aGVHD, increased Treg | Paredes ref 137 |
| Galacto-oligosaccharides (GOS) | Murine, prevented antibiotic-induced gut injury, expanded butyrogenic taxa; phase I/II NCT04373057 | Samarkhazan ref 101 (Holmes) |
| Human lysozyme goat milk | Phase I NCT04177004, recruiting | Paredes Table 2 ref 180 |
| Caveats | Prebiotics need a relatively intact microbiome; lactose may worsen GVHD by reducing diversity | Samarkhazan refs 102, 103 |

## 4. Probiotics and live biotherapeutic products

| Intervention | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| L. rhamnosus GG | Murine benefit; RCT n=31 stopped early, no change in GVHD incidence or Lactobacillus colonization | Moses refs 90, 91; Paredes ref 27; Samarkhazan ref 98 |
| L. plantarum (ACCL1633) | n=161, safe but ineffective at preventing GI GvHD; engraftment not assessable | Moses ref 92 |
| L. plantarum, paediatric | Ladas 2016, n=30, 97% compliance, no bacteraemia; phase III NCT03057054 ongoing | Samarkhazan ref 96; Paredes ref 130 |
| CBM588 (C. butyricum MIYAIRI 588) | Randomized open-label pilot, n=11 treated; higher alpha diversity, less Enterococcus and Bacteroides; too small for outcomes | Paredes ref 131 |
| B. fragilis (murine) | Improved barrier integrity, more mucus, less epithelial damage | Samarkhazan ref 97 (Sofi 2021) |
| Synbiotic before and during conditioning | Pilot RCT n=30, lower aGVHD incidence and severity, CD4+CD25+Foxp3+ Treg induction | Samarkhazan ref 95 (Yazdandoust 2023) |
| SCFA-directed strain selection | C. bolteae, Blautia spp., B. longum vary strain to strain in SCFA output; optimized selection outperformed a standard consortium of the same species in mismatched murine transplant | Moses ref 93 |
| Safety | Sepsis, bacteraemia and meningitis reported after probiotics in immunocompromised patients | Samarkhazan ref 100; Paredes ref 126 |

## 6. Ecosystem restoration, FMT

| Study | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Kakihana 2016 | n=4 steroid-resistant or dependent GI-aGVHD, 3 complete responses | Weber ref 75 |
| Van Lier | n=15, 10 complete responses (3/6 SR, 7/9 SD) | Weber ref 77 |
| Prospective SR aGVHD | n=21, ORR 52.4%; restored Clostridiales and SCFA producers, fewer Enterobacteriaceae; sustained responses rare | Moses ref 24 |
| DeFilipp | n=10, high-risk treatment-naive lower GI aGVHD plus corticosteroids, organ-specific CR 70% at day 28, correlated with tryptophan metabolites and SCFAs | Moses ref 23; Weber ref 79 |
| FMT + ruxolitinib | n=21 SR GI GvHD, ORR 71.4% at day 28, lower serum IL-2, IL-17A, CD69+ T cell fraction | Moses ref 96 |
| Li 2022 meta-analysis | 79 patients, 6 studies and 5 case reports; CR 55.9%, PR 26.5%, ORR 82.4% | Samarkhazan ref 104 |
| Prophylactic FMT RCT | n=74, capsule after neutrophil recovery; lower 4-month infection density (0.74 vs 0.91 per 100 patient-days) but numerically higher GVHD | Moses ref 97; Paredes ref 26 |
| Donor effect | All cases of severe aGVHD attributed to one of three donors, n=20 interim analysis | Moses ref 22; Paredes ref 166 |
| Engraftment predictor | Low pre-FMT diversity predicted donor strain engraftment | Paredes ref 165 |
| Safety | MDR E. coli transmitted from one donor to two allo-HCT recipients, one death (MGH); donor screening tightened | Weber ref 85; Paredes ref 151 |

## 7. Defined consortia

| Product | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| SER-155, 16 Firmicutes strains | Phase 1b, safe, durable engraftment, bloodstream infections reduced 77%, less antibiotic exposure, lower febrile neutropenia severity; NCT04995653 final analysis pending | Moses refs 98-100; Weber ref 84; Paredes refs 132, 133 |
| MaaT013, pooled donor | HERACLES phase IIa n=24 SR GI aGVHD, GI-ORR 38%; expanded access n=52, ORR 58%; responders had higher diversity, butyrate producers, SCFAs and SBAs; 5 severe infections; 6-month OS 28% prospective vs 49% expanded access | Paredes ref 167; Weber ref 78 |
| MaaT013 phase III (ARES) | NCT04769895, n=66 SR and ruxolitinib-refractory GI aGVHD, day 28 GI-ORR 62%, 12-month OS 67% responders vs 28% non-responders. ASH 2025 abstract, not peer reviewed | Paredes ref 169 |
| SER-109 | 77 Firmicutes genera, effective for recurrent C. difficile, proof of concept for defined consortia | Moses ref 101 |

## 8. Personalized and predictive

| Approach | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-transplant microbiome risk prediction | Metagenome-wide association, AUC about 0.67 for GVHD risk | Samarkhazan ref 108 |
| Donor selection by microbial profile | Proposed, not validated | Samarkhazan Challenges and future directions |
| Access disparity | FMT, probiotics and prebiotics concentrated in high-resource settings; conventional immunosuppression responds in only 40-60% | Samarkhazan refs 94, 104 |
