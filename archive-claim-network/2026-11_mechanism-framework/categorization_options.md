## Scheme Comparison

| Scheme | Categories | % unattributed | Multi-category taxa | Cats in all 4 papers |
|--------|-----------|---------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 3A | 10 | 48% | 28 | 7 |
| 3B | 9 | 31% | 31 | 5 |

| 3D | 14 | 0% | 32 | 8 |

Scheme A has the lowest category count and the sharpest mechanistic focus — it groups
by what the microbe produces — but nearly half of all statements fall into its unattributed
catch-all, limiting its utility for network analysis that requires mechanistic specificity.
Scheme B reduces that fraction to 31% by distinguishing host target from microbial function,
and it maintains clean fits for every entry. Scheme C resolves the unattributed problem most
aggressively (18%) by separating dysbiosis from pathobiont expansion at the subcategory level
and adding a dedicated therapeutic class; its two-level hierarchy supports both coarse and
fine-grained analyses but makes the 56-statement FMT/probiotic block a category unto itself.
Scheme D achieves 0% unattributed by borrowing the papers' own organizational vocabulary,
which maximizes fidelity to the source literature but makes categories heterogeneous in
their level of mechanistic abstraction. Scheme E is the only option that encodes directionality
explicitly, which is valuable for causal network construction; 24% of statements land in
"Context-dependent," reflecting genuine ambiguity rather than a failure of the scheme, and
the 2 × 4 grid is readily converted to a bipartite or signed graph structure.
