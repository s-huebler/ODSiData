Add donor relationship and HLA match to the harmonized GVHD metadata.

Follow the "Harmonization Variable Additions" section of CLAUDE.md for all
standing conventions.

SHAPE: two categorical covariates

DEFINITION: Every study records donor type as a single label that fuses two
independent things: how the donor is related to the recipient, and how well the
donor and recipient are HLA-matched. I want them split, because they act
differently — relationship drives donor availability and graft composition,
while HLA disparity is the primary driver of alloreactivity and therefore of
GVHD risk. A haploidentical transplant is related AND half-matched, which a
single fused column cannot express.

CANONICAL VOCABULARY:

  donor_relationship    related | unrelated
    related    = family donor of any degree, including HLA-identical siblings,
                 other matched family members, and haploidentical relatives.
    unrelated  = volunteer unrelated donor, OR a cord blood unit. Cord units
                 have no living related donor and are unrelated by definition —
                 see NOTES on why cord maps here rather than to its own level.

  donor_hla_match       matched | mismatched | haploidentical
    matched        = fully HLA-matched, whether related or unrelated.
    mismatched     = one or more antigen/allele mismatches, short of a
                     deliberate half-match.
    haploidentical = deliberate one-haplotype (~50%) match with a relative.
                     This is its own level, NOT a kind of "mismatched".
    NA where the source records no HLA information. Do not infer match status
    from a donor-relationship label — see stop-and-ask 1.

PLACEMENT: immediately after disease and before transplant_date, in the order
donor_relationship, donor_hla_match. These are properties of the transplant
itself rather than peri-transplant exposures, so they sit ahead of the
conditioning and prophylaxis blocks if those already exist.

NOTES:
- Cord blood maps to donor_relationship = unrelated in every study, rather than
  becoming its own level. Rationale: four of the six studies (Artacho, Ingham,
  Vallet, and DAmico by absence) already resolve cord recipients onto the
  relationship and match axes, and only Fujimoto and Liu promote cord to a donor
  category. Mapping cord to unrelated makes all six consistent. The information
  that the graft was a cord unit is not lost — it lives in the graft-source
  columns (Artacho Tranplant_Type, DAmico Stem_cell_source, Fujimoto Graft_Type,
  Ingham Ubilical.cord, Liu donor_source, Vallet csh_type), which are present in
  all six studies and are a natural next variable to harmonize. Record that in
  the log.
- haploidentical is deliberately a level of donor_hla_match, not of
  donor_relationship. A haplo donor IS a relative, so collapsing it into the
  relationship axis would make "related" mean two different things. Keeping the
  axes orthogonal is the whole point of the split.
- Do not treat haploidentical as a kind of mismatched. It is a planned half-match
  with its own prophylaxis implications, and pooling it with incidental 1-2
  antigen mismatches would blur a real distinction.
- Ingham is the only study distinguishing degree of mismatch (1 vs 2 antigens,
  n=14 and n=7). I am NOT harmonizing mismatch count in this pass since no other
  study supports it. Note in the log that Ingham's match column retains it if a
  finer variable is wanted later.

  1. Liu and Vallet HLA match. Both dictionaries gloss donor_type as
     "related = HLA-matched related donor" and "unrelated = HLA-matched
     unrelated donor". My reading is that this is descriptive prose about what
     the label typically means, not a per-patient match field, and that coding
     197 samples as "matched" on that basis would manufacture data — especially
     since Vallet includes 7 cord-blood transplants, which are rarely fully
     matched. My default is donor_hla_match = NA for both studies. Check whether
     either manuscript reports HLA matching in a patient table, and tell me if
     you find per-patient match data anywhere.
  2. Fujimoto CB. Cord units are typically 1-2 antigen mismatched, but Fujimoto
     does not state match status for its 71 CB samples. My default is NA rather
     than assuming mismatched. Tell me if the manuscript reports CB HLA matching.
  3. Liu cord inconsistency. The data dictionary states "For cord+cord
     transplants, donor_type is always cord", but 2 samples have
     donor_type = "unrelated" with donor_source = "cord+cord". Both map to
     unrelated under my rule so the harmonized value is unaffected, but tell me
     whether this is a source data error worth flagging, since it means the
     dictionary's stated invariant does not hold.
