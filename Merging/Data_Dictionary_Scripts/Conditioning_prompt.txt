Add conditioning variables to the harmonized GVHD metadata.

Follow the "Harmonization Variable Additions" section of CLAUDE.md for all
standing conventions.

SHAPE: one categorical covariate, one canonical-string covariate, two binaries

DEFINITION: Conditioning is the preparative regimen given before graft infusion,
and it is the single largest microbiome insult in the transplant course. I want
it captured on four separate axes: how intense the regimen was, which cytotoxic
drugs it contained, whether it included total body irradiation, and whether it
included serotherapy. Splitting irradiation and serotherapy out of the drug
string is deliberate — it keeps the drug combinations comparable across studies
that record irradiation and serotherapy differently, or not at all.

CANONICAL VOCABULARY:

  conditioning_intensity   MAC | RIC | NMA
    MAC = myeloablative, RIC = reduced-intensity, NMA = non-myeloablative.
    Where a study records only a two-level myeloablative flag, map 1 -> MAC and
    0 -> NMA. See NOTES for the consequence of that rule.

  conditioning_regimen     canonical drug-combination string
    Lower-case drug tokens joined by "/", sorted ALPHABETICALLY so the string is
    a deterministic join key rather than a clinical-convention name. Token set:
      bu    busulfan          (DAmico "BU", Ingham X233Bu, Liu "Bu")
      cy    cyclophosphamide  (DAmico "EDX", Ingham X270Cyclo, Liu "Cy")
      flu   fludarabine       (DAmico "FLUDARA", Ingham X284.2Fludarab, Liu "Flu")
      mel   melphalan         (DAmico "L-PAM", Ingham melphalan dose, Liu "Mel")
      thio  thiotepa          (DAmico "TT", Liu "Thio")
      treo  treosulfan        (DAmico "TREO", Liu "Treo")
      vp16  etoposide         (Ingham VP16 dose)
    TBI and serotherapy are NOT tokens in this string — they have their own
    columns. Strip them out wherever a source embeds them in a regimen name.

  conditioning_tbi         1/0
    1 = total body irradiation included in the preparative regimen.

  conditioning_serotherapy 1/0
    1 = ATG or an antibody-based agent (alemtuzumab/rituximab) given as part of
    conditioning. NA where the study does not record it at all.

PLACEMENT: immediately after sample_day_rel_transplant, in the order
conditioning_intensity, conditioning_regimen, conditioning_tbi,
conditioning_serotherapy. This puts the treatment-exposure block before the GVHD
outcome blocks. If the gvhd_prophylaxis columns already exist, conditioning goes
before them, since conditioning precedes prophylaxis chronologically.

NOTES:
- Vallet's two-level collapse has a consequence I want recorded explicitly in
  the decision log. The source documents "non myeloablative" as meaning
  "reduced-intensity OR non-myeloablative", so mapping it to NMA puts 88
  heterogeneous samples in the NMA cell. Cohort-wide that is 88 of 119 NMA
  samples, so any RIC-versus-NMA contrast is substantially a Vallet-versus-
  not-Vallet contrast. Apply the rule as specified, but log this as an open
  question with the counts.
- Do NOT derive conditioning_intensity from drug doses. I checked Ingham's doses
  against the CIBMTR/ASBMT working definitions (MAC = busulfan >9 mg/kg total,
  melphalan >=140 mg/m2, TBI >800 cGy fractionated or >500 cGy single-fraction)
  and the conversion does not hold up: melphalan's threshold is per m2 and
  Ingham has weight but no height, so BSA is not computable for the 5 melphalan
  patients; etoposide has no threshold in the criteria and 9 patients received
  it; cyclophosphamide-only regimens are not addressed; and the criteria are
  adult-derived while Ingham is paediatric (ages 1.1-16.5, weights 7-89 kg).
  Keep each study's own assertion.
- BUT add a QA table cross-checking Ingham dose against the asserted MAC, since
  it reveals a real anomaly. Busulfan is 10.0-20.0 mg/kg for all 15 dosed
  patients, comfortably myeloablative. However three patients (P23, P25, P27)
  received 200 cGy SINGLE-FRACTION TBI, below the 500 cGy single-fraction
  threshold and the canonical non-myeloablative TBI dose; all three had
  cyclophosphamide with no busulfan and no fludarabine, consistent with
  aplastic-anaemia conditioning. They are nonetheless flagged Myeloabl = 1.
  Flag them in QA and write them up as an open question. Do not change their
  conditioning_intensity.
- Artacho's Myelosuppression (0 = 98, 1 = 74) is a binary toxicity observed
  during the transplant course, not a description of the preparative regimen.
  Myelosuppression can follow either myeloablative or reduced-intensity
  conditioning. Do not map it to any conditioning column. Add a code comment
  saying so, since it is the obvious wrong answer.
- Ingham yields a single-token "flu" regimen for 5 samples, all from one SCID
  patient. Minimal or fludarabine-only conditioning is genuinely used in SCID,
  so treat this as plausible rather than a parsing failure.
- Ingham records no thiotepa (constant 0) and has no treosulfan column at all,
  so Ingham-derived regimen strings may understate the true regimen relative to
  DAmico's and Liu's parsed strings. Log this as a comparability caveat.
- Expected result of the harmonization: 17 distinct conditioning_regimen
  strings, of which 5 are shared by more than one study — cy/flu (all three:
  DAmico 5, Liu 10, Ingham 8), bu/cy/mel (DAmico 24, Ingham 23), flu (Liu 22,
  Ingham 5), bu/cy (Liu 7, Ingham 12), cy (Liu 3, Ingham 8). If your parse
  produces materially different overlap, something is wrong — check the token
  mapping before proceeding.

Before editing anything, read all six study sheets in data_dictionaries.xlsx
(the copy in Merging/, which is more current than the one in
Merging/Harmonization/) and cross-tabulate the real source columns in the
*_meta_qiime.tsv files. Two things I want your read on before you write code:

  1. DAmico intensity. The dictionary labels Conditioning_regimen as
     myeloablative, but one of the six regimens is "EDX, FLUDARA, TBI"
     (cyclophosphamide + fludarabine + TBI, n=5 samples), which in adults is
     commonly reduced-intensity. This is a paediatric cohort so it may genuinely
     be myeloablative. Check the manuscript methods if you can reach them, and
     tell me whether to assign MAC to all 104 samples or leave those 5 as NA.
  2. Liu intensity mapping. Liu uses its own three-tier scale (High /
     Intermediate / Low), which is not the ASBMT MAC/RIC/NMA scale Fujimoto
     uses. My proposed mapping is High -> MAC, Intermediate -> RIC, Low -> NMA.
     Supporting evidence: Low is Flu/TBI (n=22) and Cy/ATG (n=2), and low-dose
     Flu/TBI is the canonical non-myeloablative regimen; Intermediate is
     Flu/Cy/TBI, Flu/Cy/Thio/TBI, Flu/Mel and Flu/Treo/TBI, all
     reduced-intensity; High is Bu/Cy and Cy/TBI, classically myeloablative.
     Tell me whether you agree, and flag any case where the regimen string
     contradicts the intensity label.
